# Who earns the emissions bonus?

**Atlas Open Mathematics · Boardroom teaching case, version 1 · 28 November 2026**

A manager wants a bonus. A board wants to reduce emissions. Both have the same figures. This fictional case asks whether they would choose the same proposal and what evidence would justify changing the rule.

[Play the Boardroom challenge](https://alberttansinhon.com/math-arcade/boardroom/) · [Print the participant handout](https://alberttansinhon.com/math-arcade/boardroom/#handout) · [Join the four-week club pilot](https://alberttansinhon.com/math-arcade/club-pilot/)

## Participant sheet

The firm and its suppliers make the same quantity of the same product in every scenario. Use the same period and unit for every figure. The two columns cover all emissions in this fictional exercise, with each unit counted once. The bonus is earned when emissions from the firm’s **own operations** fall at least **25%** below the current level.

| Proposal | Own operations | Suppliers |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Current position | 80 | 20 |
| A. Upgrade equipment | 68 | 20 |
| B. Move some work to suppliers | 50 | 65 |

1. **Take the manager’s seat.** Calculate the maximum own-operations figure eligible for the bonus. Which proposal pays?
2. **Take the board’s seat.** Which proposal gives the lowest combined emissions? Calculate both changes against the current total.
3. **Rewrite the rule.** Give the bonus a measurable trigger and specify its boundary, baseline and verification evidence. Test your wording against both proposals. What would you do if a supplier’s figure were missing?

**Fresh case.** A bus operator publishes the proportion of *operated* services arriving on time. Initially, 100 were scheduled, 90 arrived on time, 10 arrived late and none was cancelled. The next period also had 100 scheduled, with 88 on time, two late and ten cancelled. Did the published measure improve? If you chose one scheduled service at random, did its chance of arriving on time improve? Give both denominators and name records you would check.

<details>
<summary><strong>Facilitator key and assessment</strong></summary>

**Bonus.** The maximum is 80 × 0.75 = 60. Proposal A cuts own emissions by 12/80 = 15%. B cuts them by 30/80 = 37.5%. Only B earns the bonus.

**Board.** The baseline total is 100. A totals 88, a 12% reduction. B totals 115, a 15% increase. B shifts emissions across the rewarded boundary. With the stated two options, A minimises total emissions at equal output.

**One workable rewrite.** Award the bonus for a reduction of at least 10% in the combined emissions associated with the same output, using the same activities, period length, units and allocation method as the 100-unit baseline. The threshold is 90. A qualifies and B does not. Reconcile dated meter and fuel records, supplier activity records, their conversion factors, purchase quantities and finished output. Explain any change in coverage or double counting. Withhold the award if the comparison cannot be verified. Ten per cent illustrates the test and has no independent scientific or legal status. Other well-specified rules can earn full credit. A 25% combined threshold, for instance, would pay neither proposal.

**Fresh case.** On-time/operated rises from 90/100 = 90% to 88/90 ≈ 97.8%. On-time/scheduled falls from 90/100 = 90% to 88/100 = 88%. Cancellations leave the published denominator. Match the timetable to dated dispatch, cancellation and arrival records for the same routes and definition of “on time”. These are probabilities per scheduled service under equal weighting. Estimating a passenger-weighted rate would also require counts of affected travellers. The figures alone do not establish why the cancellations happened.

**Observe transfer.** Before showing this key, ask the pair for both fractions and what caused the reversal. Record whether they named the changed denominator, calculated both rates and identified an appropriate independent record. Note if a hint or the key was used. A second run after seeing the key is practice. One response describes performance on this exercise.

## Run it in 20 minutes

Use the [original club guide](MATHS_ARCADE_CLUB_GUIDE.md) for the geometric and arithmetic puzzles. The Boardroom case is a second standalone session. Allocate 2 minutes to the bonus, 4 to the board, 7 to rewriting and checking the rule, 5 to the fresh case, and 2 to compare explanations. Partners swap manager and checker roles. Each group can run the material independently at no charge. Organisers who would like facilitation can request a fixed fee quoted privately before booking on the [pilot page](https://alberttansinhon.com/math-arcade/club-pilot/).

## How the four-week pilot will be assessed

The pilot opens on **28 November 2026**. The four-week review falls on **26 December 2026**, Singapore time. The intended group is two to four willing organisers. Before a session ask, *What would you otherwise run, and how much preparation would it take?* Afterwards ask what actually happened, which task caused difficulty, how much preparation was needed, and whether the organiser ran another session. Preserve refusals, abandoned sessions and missing responses as distinct outcomes.

| Decision | Evidence to examine | How to challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the website or agent work making a task easier? | Independently replay the club journey from landing page to printable case, game and feedback. Record the obstruction, version and result. | A second checker repeats the same task. Record a prevented error or a failed route even on a day without a release. |
| Is the free activity reaching groups and being reused? | Record each reported session, the number of groups known to have run it again, and what organisers would otherwise have offered. | Count an actual second delivery separately from an intention. Deduplicate repeated reports. Unreported offline use remains unknown. |
| Does the method merit further scholarly work? | Record a specific independent use, adaptation, critique or correction, the claim concerned and a retrievable source or reproducible check. | An adverse result and a critical citation are eligible. Read the citing passage. Self-posted cross-links and agent-generated mentions do not establish independent use. |
| Could a pair apply the distinction to a fresh case unaided? | Record anonymised group-level descriptions of unaided fresh-case reasoning, its denominator and its verification evidence. | Separate unable, partial, supported, unobserved and previously exposed responses. Consider an organiser’s reported alternative activity. A few voluntary cases cannot establish a causal effect. |
| Is facilitated delivery worth continuing? | Record cash actually collected minus refunds and direct cash costs, alongside total preparation, delivery and follow-up hours. Divide net cash by total hours when total hours exceed zero. | Enquiries and unpaid invoices belong in separate columns. A free club can be educationally valuable. If cash or time is unknown, leave the effective hourly return undetermined. |

At review, consider the evidence for open teaching materials, paid delivery and a scholarly paper separately. Each decision can go a different way. Preserve the individual case descriptions privately. Publish only a sufficiently anonymised summary if the small group allows it.
